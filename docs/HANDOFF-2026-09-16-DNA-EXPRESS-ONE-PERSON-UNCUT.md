# HANDOFF — 2026-09-16 — DNA EXPRESS: ONE-PERSON UNCUT

Status: binding editorial handoff after the 15–16 September customer interview.

## 1. Core product definition
DNA EXPRESS is a professional music magazine made for one reader, but it must NEVER read as a personalized newsletter. Internally Music DNA selects and prioritizes; externally the magazine writes as if it serves a mass readership.

Working quality metaphor: **a one-person Uncut**, with useful visual/editorial influences from **Uncut + Classic Rock + Prog**.

- Uncut: journalistic backbone, readable long-form, archive/context.
- Classic Rock: rock energy, catalog imagery, memorabilia, live/tour world.
- Prog: occasional cinematic visual ambition.
- The benchmark is professional music-magazine quality, not a styled report or dashboard.
- A page/article that would visibly look or read out of place inside a top professional music magazine is not publishable.

## 2. Language and reader model
- Primary language: Dutch. Translate useful interviews from languages the reader does not command; summaries are welcome.
- Write for an average interested music-magazine reader, NOT for a musician or musicologist.
- Concrete, fluent, adult prose. No vague/philosophical wallpaper, no grandiloquent filler, no Music-DNA jargon in editorial copy.
- Technical musical analysis is NOT a reader interest. Do not tell the reader which instrument enters at what second unless genuinely essential to the story.
- The reader is primarily interested in music, bands, albums, history, stories, catalog, artwork and meaningful connections.
- The human/private side of artists may be covered broadly only when relevant and well sourced; it is not a primary editorial obsession and DNA EXPRESS is not a gossip magazine.
- Functional humor is welcome; no forced jokes.

## 3. Editorial autonomy
The editorial team may independently:
- scan current music news each week and select relevant stories;
- publish artists/albums not yet in Music DNA when the fit/story is promising;
- mix new and old music without quota;
- let story quality determine article length and total issue length;
- follow relevant side paths (producer, studio, place, label, history, connections, etc.);
- preserve Saturday surprise: do not pre-announce contents unless essential reader-only information is missing;
- publish well-labelled rumors/speculation when newsworthy and sourced;
- publish historical stories without a current peg;
- use anniversaries as a research trigger, never as automatic publication justification;
- occasionally publish a superb wildcard story outside the obvious taste area;
- revisit earlier stories only when there is genuinely new value;
- split an exceptionally rich story over two issues only for truly top-tier material/bands and when both parts stand on their own.

No bonus articles merely because material exists.

## 4. Main-story selection
Daily Music-DNA ratings are editorial signals.
- **RAAK** is a strong reason to investigate an artist immediately; it is not an automatic cover assignment.
- One **NEE** rejects a track, not an artist.
- Two clear misses by the same artist are a strong signal that the work probably fits poorly; recommendation threshold rises, but the artist is not journalistically banned.
- Existing favorite bands are a rich source for substantial second features and can become cover stories when the actual story warrants it.
- A new discovery can outrank an established favorite when the story, timing and material are stronger.

## 5. Visual doctrine — binding
DNA EXPRESS must be visually exceptional on the iMac. Use the available widescreen canvas deliberately.

The reader's strongest visual interest is the **visual world of the music/band**, not portrait photography for its own sake. Prioritize:
- album and single artwork;
- catalog visuals;
- band logos and typography;
- stage design, lighting, effects, props and tour concepts;
- tour posters and promotional artwork;
- booklets, labels, advertisements and memorabilia;
- authentic archive documents, maps and historical imagery when relevant;
- band/live photography when it materially adds value.

**Hard authenticity rule:** whenever a real artist/band/person is depicted as that person, use only authentic photography of the actual person/band. NEVER use AI-generated lookalikes or invented band members. If authenticity cannot be established, do not use the portrait.

AI/generated imagery may never masquerade as documentary evidence. Generic atmosphere is not a substitute for catalog/archive material.

Rich illustration is a core differentiator. The visual test is that page turns should regularly create a genuine 'wow/kicken' response.

## 6. Fixed structure versus freedom
The reader likes structure. DNA EXPRESS therefore needs recurring anchors around free-form journalism.

Recurring editorial spine should include:
- a **main artist/cover story**;
- a **second substantial artist/band story** from the interest area;
- **Music DNA** weekly analysis/duiding;
- relevant **current news radar** for favorites, RAAK artists and promising artists;
- **ON THE ROAD!** live/tour radar;
- selective new-album/release reviews;
- a balance of present and past, without a fixed quota.

Fixed rubrics provide recognition; they must never force weak material onto pages.

## 7. Music DNA analysis — methodological rule
The reader explicitly rejects trivial or presumptuous taste conclusions.

- Small samples = observations/signals only.
- Early patterns must be phrased as hypotheses/suspected patterns, not conclusions.
- Stronger conclusions require a substantial longitudinal evidence base.
- Use statistically accepted principles where applicable; state sample size/limitations when relevant.
- Count counter-evidence. Correlation is not causation.
- 'We do not know yet' is a valid result.
- Build longitudinal analysis across weeks, months, quarters and years.
- Compare ratings with external structured data when reliable: release year, country, AllMusic genre/style, artist relations, catalog and other defensible datasets.
- The long-term playful research goal is the reader's 'Heilige Graal': discovering which combinations genuinely predict unusually high RAAK probability. Do not engineer the result in advance.
- Data may contradict the reader's self-image; publish the evidence cautiously and transparently.

## 8. Reviews
Reviews are welcome in the reader's established or high-potential taste area.
- Prefer a small, strongly filtered set of relevant new releases.
- Physical reissues are low-interest because the reader does not collect LP/CD; only cover a reissue when it adds genuine musical/journalistic value.
- One review is one private opinion, not authority.
- Consensus across multiple serious reviewers is useful signal; explain where reviewers converge/diverge.
- DNA EXPRESS may have an editorial opinion when reasoned, but never present it as the reader's opinion.

## 9. Interviews and sourcing
- Use interviews actively.
- Translate/summarize interesting foreign-language interviews into Dutch.
- Always identify the original interviewer/publication/date where relevant.
- Never imply DNA EXPRESS conducted an interview unless it actually did.
- Multiple interviews may be synthesized into a richer sourced narrative.
- Facts must be verified. Rumors/speculation may be published when clearly labelled and sourced.

## 10. History, sidebars and albums
- History provides context in proportion to the story; it is not a compulsory lesson.
- Sidebars/timelines/mini-stories are welcome only when they add real information or visual value; never fill layout holes.
- Albums may themselves be full cover-story subjects: creation, artwork, reception, tour, catalog position, historical context, influence.
- Discoveries can run backward: old unknown albums are as valid as new releases.
- Music documentaries, concert films, books, podcasts and exhibitions are selectively eligible when directly relevant to the music world.
- Music-industry mechanics are eligible when they concretely explain what happens to relevant music/bands.

## 11. Listening service
Use a compact **LUISTEREN / BEGIN HIER** service when useful:
- 3–5 carefully chosen tracks or one album;
- invite, do not assign homework;
- no technical listening exercises;
- the ideal effect is: the article makes the reader want to open Spotify immediately.

## 12. ON THE ROAD!
Live touring is a recurring high-interest service.
- Focus on bands in the interest area and promising Music-DNA artists.
- Geographic relevance is practical, not national: Netherlands, Belgium, northern France and potentially nearby Germany can all matter from Zeeland.
- Interest level affects acceptable travel distance; top bands justify a wider radius.
- Festivals get only brief signalling because the reader does not plan to attend festivals.
- Maintain background knowledge of concerts already attended when known; use only when editorially useful.
- ON THE ROAD! should be a curated radar, not a giant agenda. Study strong examples from established music publications before locking presentation.
