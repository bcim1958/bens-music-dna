# DNA EXPRESS — Visual-World Source Network 1.3

Date: 2026-09-15
Status: ACTIVE SUPPORTING SOURCE NETWORK

## Purpose
Extend the international journalism network beyond music news into the visual and physical worlds behind the music: album art, illustration, photography, posters, stage design, recording studios, locations, objects, ephemera and institutional archives.

Core editorial rule:
**Do not search first for a picture of the musician. Search first for the world the music inhabits.**

This network supports DNA EXPRESS Art Direction 2.0 and the accepted priority: mythology/world → symbols/objects → places/environment → action/live → musician portrait.

## A. Album art / graphic design — primary and high-value sources

### Artist/designer primary sources
- Roger Dean official site — primary source for paintings, drawings, logos, architectural designs and the YES/Asia visual world; exceptionally high fit for prog/art-rock research.
- Official YES site — useful cross-check for current/historical Roger Dean commissions and the role of his artwork in YES identity.
- Pink Floyd official archive / Storm Thorgerson biography — primary band-side provenance for Hipgnosis/Storm Thorgerson and Pink Floyd visual history.
- Metallica Black Box / The Art of Metallica — official band archive containing drafts of album art, shirt imagery, gig posters, storyboards and artist work including Pushead and Tony Squindo.
- Artist/band official sites and official deluxe/reissue pages — first route when artwork credits, booklet scans, visualizers, designers or photographers are documented.

### Institutional design archives
- V&A Explore the Collections — searchable >1 million catalogue records and >500k object images; valuable for graphic design, fashion, photography, performance and album-related objects.
- V&A Archive of Art & Design — designs, sketches, correspondence, photographs, promotional material and graphic-design ephemera; deep research route rather than weekly quick radar.
- V&A David Bowie collection — photographs, album sleeve artwork, memorabilia and tour/set design; model example of artist-world research across music, design and theatre.
- Rock & Roll Hall of Fame Library & Archives — album-cover prints, posters, flyers, personal papers, photographs, label/business records and research guides.
- Museum of Pop Culture (MoPOP) Online Collection Vault — selected access to an 85,000+ object permanent collection; instruments, artifacts, visual culture and music history.

## B. Music photography / image archives

### Core international
- Rockarchive — founded by Jill Furmanovsky; >1,000 images by 80+ photographers/designers; strong UK/international rock history plus graphic art including Storm Thorgerson.
- Jill Furmanovsky Archive — direct photographer archive beginning in 1972; Led Zeppelin, Pink Floyd, Clash, Joy Division, Blondie/Debbie Harry, Rolling Stones, Oasis and many more. Useful for photographer-level provenance and editorial/licensing awareness.
- Rock & Roll Hall of Fame photographic collections — historical/archival context, especially when tied to specific collections, tours, promoters or artists.
- MoPOP — visual artifacts and photography, especially US/Seattle/counterculture and artist-object context.

### Scandinavia — HIGH PRIORITY VISUAL SOURCE
- Rockshot (Stockholm) — Scandinavia's largest specialist rock/pop photo archive; 2M+ photographs, 25+ Swedish rock photographers, 1950s–2010s. Very high value for Swedish/Scandinavian artist-world research and historical imagery.

Operational rule: for Scandinavian artists, search Rockshot and local photographer credits before defaulting to Getty-style generic portrait results.

## C. Concert posters / print culture / ephemera
- Concert Collect — structured concert-poster archive recording art, artist/designer, editions/variants, venue and show; useful for visual vocabulary and designer discovery.
- Rock & Roll Hall of Fame Library & Archives — posters, flyers, tour papers and promoter collections.
- VERA Art Division / VERA poster history — Dutch model of sustained hand-screenprinted gig-poster culture since 1974; useful for punk/alternative/DIY visual language and Netherlands historical context.
- The Metropolitan Museum of Art rock-poster material — museum-level contextual source for the evolution of rock posters from functional advertising into a distinct visual art form.
- Artist/venue/promoter official archives — preferred when reconstructing a specific concert's real poster/ticket/handbill.

## D. Stage design / lighting / live-world sources
- Rock & Roll Hall of Fame Jules Fisher Papers — exceptional primary archival route for 1970s stage and lighting design: plots, elevations, fabric samples, stage drawings, direction notes and photographs, including David Bowie and Rolling Stones material.
- V&A performance/design collections — stage/set/costume/design research when artist-specific holdings exist.
- Official tour production credits, designers' studios and production companies — primary route for current stage, lighting, video-wall, costume and scenic design.
- Official band behind-the-scenes/tour books — use for verified visual production context rather than generic concert photography.

Weekly rule: if a live story's stage world is editorially meaningful, identify the designer/lighting/scenic/video team where practical instead of treating the musician as the only visual author.

## E. Recording studios / physical places — primary sources

### UK
- Abbey Road Studios — official history, room histories, equipment/engineering context and artist/session heritage. High value for Beatles, Pink Floyd, Deep Purple, prog/classic rock and recording-technology stories.
- Rockfield Studios — official studio source; Rush, Black Sabbath, Queen, Opeth, Royal Blood, Oasis and others. Particularly high Music-DNA relevance because it connects several of Ben's core worlds.
- Royal Commission on the Ancient and Historical Monuments of Wales (Rockfield documentation) — independent heritage/context source for Rockfield's development and early rock clientele.

### Germany
- Hansa Studios Berlin — official history and preserved spaces; Bowie/Iggy Pop, U2, Depeche Mode, Killing Joke, Siouxsie and the Banshees, The Hives and others. Very high value for Berlin/new-wave/post-punk/rock visual-place stories.

### United States
- Electric Lady Studios — official history and place provenance from Jimi Hendrix onward; useful for New York studio-world stories and classic rock recording history.

Routing rule: studio/location pages are not merely fact sources. They can be visual-world sources: architecture, rooms, equipment, streets, geography, industrial/urban/rural setting and the human working environment may become editorial material.

## F. Artist-world source ladder
For any substantial DNA EXPRESS artist story, research in this order where applicable:
1. official artist/band visual archive and current release credits;
2. album/booklet/reissue credits: designer, illustrator, photographer, art director;
3. creator's own site/archive;
4. museum/institutional archive;
5. specialist photography/poster archive;
6. studio/venue/location primary source;
7. high-quality journalism/interview with creator;
8. fan/archive source only as a lead when stronger provenance is unavailable.

Record creator names. A visual should not become anonymous merely because the band is famous.

## G. What to extract — visual DNA record
For each headliner/substantial subject, the evidence pack may record:
- palette/colour behavior;
- typography attitude;
- recurring symbols/mascots/characters;
- objects/relics/props;
- landscapes/cities/buildings/rooms;
- historical period references;
- materials/textures/printing processes;
- lighting/weather/atmosphere;
- stage/scenic vocabulary;
- album/booklet/poster lineage;
- designer/illustrator/photographer/art director;
- studio/location associations;
- what is canonical vs later reinterpretation;
- source/provenance for every non-obvious claim.

The record is inspiration/evidence, not a command to copy an existing cover.

## H. High-priority Music-DNA visual routes

### Progressive / art rock
Roger Dean → V&A → Rockarchive → artist official archive → studio/location.
Use for YES/Asia and adjacent visual-world thinking; Genesis/Pink Floyd/Steven Wilson require their own creator-specific routes rather than a generic prog look.

### Classic hard rock / heavy metal
Official band archives → credited cover artist → Rock Hall/MoPOP → poster archive → stage-production credits → studio/location.
Especially useful for Iron Maiden/Eddie, Judas Priest, Metal Church, KISS, Metallica and visual-symbol systems.

### Punk / post-punk / new wave
V&A → Rock Hall → Rockarchive/Jill Furmanovsky → Janette Beckman/MoPOP material → gig-poster/DIY archives → Hansa/venue/city sources.
Prioritize print culture, streets, venues, flyers and subculture over polished portraits.

### Scandinavia
Artist/label → credited Nordic creator → Rockshot → local museum/archive/venue → Scandinavian music journalism → international archive.
Do not let Swedish/Finnish/Norwegian/Danish visual history be flattened into Anglo-American stock imagery.

## I. Source-quality and rights/provenance fields
For visual-world candidates record separately:
- provenance confidence;
- creator/credit confidence;
- date/era confidence;
- whether image/object is original, reproduction or later reinterpretation;
- whether it is being used as research reference, factual evidence, or actual publication asset;
- licensing/use status when actual asset use matters.

Research relevance does not imply publication permission. Conversely, rights uncertainty should not prevent private research into the visual language.

## K. Verified anchor findings behind v1.3
- Rockshot identifies itself as Scandinavia's largest rock/pop photo archive and reports 2M+ images from 25+ Swedish rock photographers.
- Rockarchive reports >1,000 images by 80+ photographers/designers and direct relationships with music photographers.
- V&A Explore the Collections exposes >1M catalogue records and >500k object images; its Archive of Art & Design includes designs, sketches, correspondence, photographs and promotional material.
- Rock Hall Library & Archives explicitly holds photographs, posters/flyers, personal papers, label/business records, magazines/fanzines/newspapers and original recordings; its Jules Fisher holdings include stage/lighting production documents.
- MoPOP reports >85,000 permanent-collection objects with a growing online vault.
- Abbey Road, Rockfield, Hansa and Electric Lady maintain official historical material tying physical spaces to major recordings/artists.
- Metallica's official Black Box includes an Art of Metallica exhibit with drafts, album art, gig posters, storyboards and named visual artists.
