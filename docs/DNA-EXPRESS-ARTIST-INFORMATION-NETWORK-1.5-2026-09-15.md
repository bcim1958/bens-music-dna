# DNA EXPRESS — Artist Information Network 1.5

Date: 2026-09-15
Status: ACTIVE EDITORIAL INFRASTRUCTURE / EXPANSION LAYER

## Goal
Make DNA EXPRESS capable of finding both current and historical information about an artist through multiple independent routes, instead of depending on one kind of music journalism.

This extends the International Source Network, Americas expansions, Visual-World Source Network and Creator/Magazine Index. It does not replace those layers.

Core principle:
**Search the artist as an ecosystem, not merely as a name in the press.**

An artist entity may connect to:
artist/band ↔ members ↔ releases ↔ label(s) ↔ management ↔ booking agent ↔ promoter ↔ venues/festivals ↔ producer/studio ↔ designer/illustrator/photographer ↔ tour/stage production ↔ press/radio/video/podcast/archive ↔ scenes/locations ↔ related artists.

## 1. Current journalism radar
Retain the accepted geographic and genre-routing network. For current claims prefer original reporting, direct interviews and primary announcements over rewrites. Deduplicate press-release syndication.

## 2. Historical journalism and press archives
Priority routes:
- Rock's Backpages — full-text historical music journalism plus audio interviews; strong artist/publication/date search.
- RILM Archive of Popular Music Magazines (RAPMM) — digitised independent music magazines/fanzines.
- World Radio History — historical radio, music-business and trade publications where available.
- RoJaRo — article/review/discography index and discovery route.
- publication-specific archives and scans: NME, Melody Maker, Sounds, Record Mirror, Creem, Crawdaddy, Trouser Press, BRAVO, etc., subject to access and verification.
- national libraries, newspaper archives, museum/cultural-heritage collections.

FLASHBACK rule: reconstruct what was knowable then before applying hindsight.

## 3. Radio and session layer
Search when artist history, live development or contemporary voice matters:
- BBC programme/session/archive routes, including historic radio sessions and interviews where accessible;
- national/public broadcasters in artist home countries;
- specialist rock/metal/prog radio archives and station interviews;
- radio-industry archives through World Radio History;
- official artist/label uploads of historic sessions.

Treat programme metadata, broadcast date and recording date separately.

## 4. Podcasts and long-form audio
Build a candidate radar around:
- established music-journalism podcasts;
- journalist-hosted long interviews;
- artist/producer/manager interviews;
- official label/venue/festival podcasts;
- Rock's Backpages audio archive/podcast.

Editorial rule: an original long-form interview can be a primary testimony source; a commentary podcast repeating news is only secondary.

## 5. Video journalism and documentaries
Search:
- official artist and label channels;
- serious music publications' interview/documentary channels;
- broadcaster archives;
- venue/festival session channels;
- studio/producer channels;
- documentary catalogues and museum archives.

Always distinguish original interview/performance footage from retrospective commentary and fan edits.

## 6. Music-business trade press
Add a permanent industry layer:
- Music Business Worldwide — global label, management, rights, executive and business intelligence.
- Music Week — UK music business, labels, management, charts, touring and industry.
- Billboard — charts plus label/business/touring context.
- Pollstar — live-industry editorial/data, artist routing, venue/promoter and box-office history where accessible.
- IQ Magazine — international live-music business: promoters, managers, venues, festivals, arenas, touring markets.
- relevant national music-industry bodies and trade publications.

Use this layer for the question: **why did this release, signing, tour or business change happen?**

## 7. Labels — REQUIRED ARTIST RELATION
Every significant artist record should be able to carry time-bounded label relationships.

Fields:
- label name;
- parent/imprint where relevant;
- territory if deal differs by region;
- start/end period or release span;
- releases associated;
- official label artist/roster page;
- catalogue/reissue/archive links;
- source and verification date.

Search order:
1. artist official source;
2. label official source/catalogue;
3. release credits/booklet;
4. authoritative discography/business source;
5. journalism.

Do not assume the current label released the historic catalogue.

## 8. Management — REQUIRED CURRENT RELATION WHEN FINDABLE
Management is a first-class relationship, not a footnote.

Fields:
- management company;
- named manager(s) when publicly professional information;
- role;
- start/end or known-as-of date;
- official management roster/profile;
- artist confirmation if available;
- booking/press contacts kept separate;
- source and verification date.

Management changes are time-sensitive. Never present an old manager as current without a current check.

Useful discovery/verification routes include artist official contacts, management-company rosters, label/press releases, trade press such as MBW/Music Week/IQ, and professional festival/venue biographies.

## 9. Booking agents and promoters
Store separately from management:
- agency;
- agent by territory where public/professional;
- promoter/tour promoter;
- tour/festival relationship;
- valid-as-of date.

This helps explain tour geography and provides additional current artist information.

Venues are both current-programme sources and historical archives.

Priority venue families include:

- Melkweg, Amsterdam.
- TivoliVredenburg and historical Tivoli/Vredenburg, Utrecht.
- AFAS Live, Amsterdam.
- Ziggo Dome, Amsterdam.
- 013, Tilburg.
- Effenaar, Eindhoven.
- Doornroosje, Nijmegen.
- Hedon, Zwolle.
- De Oosterpoort / SPOT, Groningen.
- Paard, The Hague.
- Patronaat, Haarlem.
- Metropool, Hengelo/Enschede.
- Neushoorn, Leeuwarden.
- Muziekgieterij, Maastricht.
- Gebouw-T / De Maagd, Bergen op Zoom where relevant.
- De Piek, Vlissingen.

Use official venue programme/archive first for announced concerts, exact dates, support acts and local editorial context.

### Belgium

### Germany

### Luxembourg

### France / northern France
- Lille and Paris venues become active when tour geography or artist history warrants it; use official venue/promoter sources rather than maintaining an indiscriminate fixed list.

### UK as near-European historical/touring layer
Key venue/festival archives should be activated for UK artists and touring history: Hammersmith/Odeon-Apollo lineage, Marquee history, Roundhouse, Royal Albert Hall, Wembley, Download/Donington and artist-specific local venues.

Venue rule: a venue page can be authoritative for its own programme/date/location, but not automatically for broader artist biography.

## 11. Festivals as scene snapshots
Festivals can expose:
- exact billing hierarchy;
- support/peer relationships;
- scene clusters;
- tour timing;
- local interviews;
- photographs/video;
- programme books/posters.

Store festival appearance as event evidence, not as proof of musical similarity by itself.

## 12. Instrument, recording and production press
Expand systematic routes for musicianship and sound:
- Guitar World / Guitar Player / Guitarist;
- Bass Player and specialist bass sources;
- Modern Drummer and drum-specialist press;
- Sound On Sound — recording/production/studio technique;
- Mix and relevant professional audio archives;
- Keyboard/synth specialist archives when relevant;
- producer/engineer official interviews and studio histories.

Useful for Rush, Genesis, Maiden, Priest, prog, hard rock and production-heavy stories where ordinary press is shallow.

## 13. Creator and visual-world relation
Continue the 1.3/1.4 network:
artist ↔ album/era ↔ artwork/visual identity ↔ designer/illustrator/photographer ↔ official creator archive ↔ museum/archive ↔ stage/poster/studio/location.

Specific creator networks remain artist/era-specific; never assign one creator to an entire career without evidence.

## 14. Artist Information Pack — default retrieval object
For a significant DNA EXPRESS candidate, construct an internal pack as needed:
- identity and aliases;
- origin/scene;
- current members + historical members;
- current status;
- current label + historical labels;
- current management;
- booking/promoter where useful;
- discography/recent release;
- current tour/official live dates;
- venue/festival evidence;
- recent interviews/news;
- historical press/interviews;
- radio/video/podcast testimony;
- producer/studio;
- visual creators/world;
- related artists and real-world links;
- Music DNA taxonomy and personal relevance;
- unresolved contradictions;
- source timestamps.

Not every magazine story needs every field. The pack is a retrieval capability, not compulsory page furniture.

## 15. Time-awareness / stale-data guard
Relationships that can change must carry a temporal state:
- CURRENT — verified recently;
- HISTORICAL — ended or belongs to a defined era;
- AS-OF DATE — last verified date known;
- UNCERTAIN — conflicting/insufficient evidence.

Apply especially to members, label, management, agent, promoter, tour, venue dates and release status.

## 16. Source hierarchy by question
There is no universal best source.

- New release/date → artist + label primary.
- Management/signing/business → artist/management + trade press.
- Concert announcement → artist + venue/promoter.
- Historical concert → venue archive + contemporary press + setlist lead/cross-check.
- What artist said → original interview/audio/video.
- Critical reception → contemporary reviews, preserve disagreement.
- Instrument/production → musician/producer/engineer + specialist technical press.
- Visual world → credits + creator archive + museum/photo archive.
- Exact-week history → contemporary dated sources before retrospectives.

## 17. Failure and contradiction behavior
If the network cannot verify a claim:
- do not fill the gap by inference;
- mark unresolved;
- search another medium, geography or relationship node;
- distinguish absence of evidence from evidence of absence.

## 18. Editorial discovery route
For any artist, DNA EXPRESS can now fan out from the artist name through at least these independent doors:
1. official artist;
2. current/historical music journalism;
3. label;
4. management;
5. booking/promoter;
6. venue/festival;
7. trade press;
8. radio/session;
9. podcast/audio interview;
10. video/documentary;
11. instrument/production press;
12. studio/producer;
13. creator/visual archive;
14. historical library/archive;
15. scene/local-country press.

The purpose is resilience: when one route is silent, another route can still reveal the story.

## 19. Database direction
The long-term knowledge graph should support typed, time-bounded edges rather than flattening these facts into prose:
- ARTIST_SIGNED_TO_LABEL
- ARTIST_MANAGED_BY
- ARTIST_BOOKED_BY
- ARTIST_PLAYED_VENUE
- ARTIST_PLAYED_FESTIVAL
- ARTIST_RECORDED_AT
- RELEASE_PRODUCED_BY
- RELEASE_ARTWORK_BY
- ARTIST_PHOTOGRAPHED_BY
- TOUR_PROMOTED_BY
- ARTIST_INTERVIEWED_IN
- ARTIST_BROADCAST_ON
- ARTIST_REVIEWED_IN

Each edge should retain source, date/period, confidence/status and territory when relevant.

## 20. Governance
This layer makes label, management and venue intelligence first-class parts of Music DNA / DNA EXPRESS research.

It does not mean every possible website is scanned every week. The weekly production protocol routes queries through the relevant doors based on artist, geography, genre, story and time period.

Closing rule:
**For an artist, never ask only 'what does the press say?'. Ask who released them, who represents them, where they played, who recorded and designed them, what they said themselves, what people wrote at the time, and what the surviving evidence says now.**
