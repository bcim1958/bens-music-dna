# DNA EXPRESS — Creator Network + Magazine Index 1.4

Date: 2026-09-15
Status: ACTIVE SUPPORTING RESEARCH LAYER

## Purpose
Extend the International Source Network and Visual-World Source Network with two linked discovery systems:
1. creator relationships behind important visual music worlds;
2. a magazine-discovery index seeded by the Wikipedia List of music magazines supplied by Ben.

The Wikipedia list is a DISCOVERY INDEX, not an authority list. It is explicitly incomplete and mixes active, defunct, broad, specialist and historical publications. Every useful candidate must be independently checked before promotion into Tier A/B or Flashback use.

## Creator relationship model
Canonical research relation:
**artist ↔ release/era ↔ visual world ↔ creator/designer/illustrator/photographer ↔ studio/location/stage ↔ primary/archival source**

For each substantial DNA EXPRESS story, capture creator credit and provenance where useful. Never infer an artist from visual similarity alone.

## Priority creator networks

### Iron Maiden ↔ Eddie ↔ Derek Riggs
Research purpose: one of the strongest examples of a recurring character becoming a band's visual mythology.
Route:
- Iron Maiden official archive/merch/reissue material for canonical release context;
- Derek Riggs official portfolio/site and direct interviews for creator-side provenance;
- album/booklet credits and label material;
- Rockarchive / museum or exhibition catalogues for contextual photography/design history;
- specialist press only after creator/official evidence for specific art claims.
Editorial use: Eddie evolution, era-specific worlds, symbols/objects, visual continuity vs reinvention.

### Ghost ↔ Papas/characters ↔ Zbigniew Bielak + other credited creators
Research purpose: Ghost's world is character-, ritual-, typography-, costume- and cover-driven and should not be reduced to Tobias Forge portraits.
Route:
- Ghost official release/merch/tour sources;
- release booklet/label credits for exact artwork attribution;
- Zbigniew Bielak's creator channels/portfolio/interviews where the relevant work is explicitly credited;
- costume/stage/photo credits when a story depends on them;
- visual-art and metal press as context, not substitute for credit evidence.
Editorial use: Papal succession, sacred/profane iconography, architectural/religious visual grammar, stage ritual, cover-era distinctions.
Guardrail: credit each Ghost visual era separately; do not assume one creator made all Ghost imagery.

### Judas Priest ↔ metallic/futurist iconography ↔ Mark Wilkinson and other credited sleeve creators
Verified creator route: Mark Wilkinson's official site documents his album-art practice and Judas Priest metal landscapes. Painkiller is credited to Wilkinson in standard release documentation.
Route:
- Judas Priest official/reissue/booklet credits;
- Mark Wilkinson official archive/site for his credited work;
- label archives and physical-edition credits;
- museum/design sources for broader context.
Editorial use: machines, chrome, wings, emblems, speed, heroic metal object-worlds.
Guardrail: Judas Priest's visual history spans multiple creators; never label the whole catalogue 'Mark Wilkinson'.

### Genesis ↔ A Trick of the Tail / Wind & Wuthering ↔ Hipgnosis + Colin Elgie
Verified starting point: A Trick of the Tail sleeve is credited to Hipgnosis and Colin Elgie; its illustrated characters correspond to song worlds, making it a model case for DNA EXPRESS world-first visual research.
Route:
- physical/booklet/reissue credits and Genesis official material;
- Hipgnosis/Storm Thorgerson/Aubrey Powell archival material;
- Colin Elgie creator/archive material where available;
- specialist prog/design scholarship for interpretation after credits are locked.
Editorial use: Squonk and other storybook figures, literary illustration, parchment/engraving atmosphere, narrative characters instead of band portraits.

### KISS ↔ personas ↔ logo/costume/stage/merch creator chain
Research purpose: KISS is not one cover artist; its visual DNA is a system of personas, makeup, logo, costumes, stage spectacle, photography, comics and merchandise.
Route:
- KISS official history/archive and release credits;
- original logo provenance and creator interviews/documents;
- costume/stage designer credits per tour/era;
- photographers and poster archives;
- museum/auction catalogues as object provenance leads, corroborated where consequential.
Editorial use: Demon/Starchild/Spaceman/Catman as character system, spectacle engineering, logo/typography, stage objects.
Guardrail: separate member-created identity elements from commissioned design work.

### Metal Church ↔ album-cover symbol systems
Research purpose: Ben has explicitly responded strongly to Metal Church's gothic/religious/object-heavy visual language.
Route:
- physical release/booklet credits first;
- label/reissue documentation;
- identified illustrator/designer's own archive;
- specialist interviews and collector scans only as leads where primary credits are unavailable.
Editorial use: church architecture, crosses/relics, ruined sanctuary, metallic/gothic object language.
Guardrail: current creator mapping is NOT complete; do not assign a creator without release-level evidence.

## Magazine candidate promotion test
A magazine found through Wikipedia or any directory enters the working network only after checking:
1. Is it active, archived, or defunct?
2. What country/language/scene did it actually cover?
3. Does it overlap Ben's Music DNA or a useful adventure boundary?
4. Does it publish original reporting/interviews/reviews or mostly rewrites?
5. Can issue/article dates be established?
6. Is material accessible enough to support weekly production or historical research?
7. For historical claims, can the actual issue/page or a trustworthy archival reproduction be located?

Classification after checking:
- CORE RADAR;
- REGIONAL/SPECIALIST;
- HISTORICAL/FLASHBACK;
- DISCOVERY LEAD ONLY;
- REJECT / NO CURRENT VALUE.

## New source routes worth systematic exploration
The Wikipedia index reveals useful source families that the existing network should not overlook:

### Instrument/player press
Guitar World, Guitar Player, Guitarist and comparable bass/drum/keyboard press can contain long-form musician interviews, recording details, technique and gear history absent from general music news.
Use for Rush, Maiden, Priest, prog and guitar-led hard rock when the story is musical rather than celebrity-led.

### Collector/reissue press
Goldmine and similar collector publications can help with release variants, reissues, archival interviews and object history. Cross-check catalogue facts against labels/physical credits/Discogs/MusicBrainz as appropriate.

### National historical press
Use magazine directories to discover country-specific extinct publications, then route to national libraries, Internet Archive holdings, Google Books where lawful/available, museum collections, university archives and collector indexes.

### Youth/pop culture press
Do not dismiss BRAVO, Smash Hits or comparable titles merely because they were not 'serious rock press'. For a Flashback they may reveal how an artist was actually presented to readers at that moment, which can contrast powerfully with retrospective rock mythology.

## Weekly integration
When a major artist/story enters the candidate pool:
1. identify origin/scene and current local press;
2. identify relevant historical magazines by era/country;
3. identify visual creator credits at release/era level;
4. search creator-side and archive-side evidence;
5. identify studio/location/stage sources where they add story value;
6. only then assemble DNA EXPRESS visual/story world.

For Flashback:
**date window → contemporary magazine candidates → actual issue/article evidence → reconstruct what was knowable then → only afterwards add EN TOEN… hindsight.**
