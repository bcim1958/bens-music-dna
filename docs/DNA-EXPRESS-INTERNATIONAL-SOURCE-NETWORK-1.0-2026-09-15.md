# DNA EXPRESS — International Source Network 1.0

Date: 2026-09-15
Status: ACTIVE SUPPORTING SOURCE NETWORK / EXPERIMENTAL WEIGHTING

## Purpose
Expand the accepted 2026-09-12 source map into an international editorial listening network for DNA EXPRESS and Music DNA.

The objective is not to scan hundreds of sites indiscriminately. The objective is to make sure DNA EXPRESS looks in the parts of the world where Ben's music actually lives, including strong local-language journalism that may surface stories before or better than English-language aggregators.

Core rule:
**First ask whether we looked in the right music world; then ask whether the individual claim is sufficiently sourced.**

Language is not a relevance filter. Useful Swedish, Finnish, Norwegian, Danish, German, French, Italian, Spanish, Polish or Japanese material may be summarized editorially in Dutch. Never silently treat a machine-translated nuance as a verified quote.

## Primary factual layer — ALWAYS AVAILABLE
These are not journalism competitors; they answer different questions.
- official artist/band site and verified channels;
- official label;
- official venue/festival/promoter;
- setlist.fm for live-history leads/cross-checking;
- AllMusic for taxonomy/career/relationship backbone;
- MusicBrainz/Discogs where release/credit identity needs additional cross-checking (not sole authority for consequential claims);
- Spotify for operational identity/playability, not historical authority;
- chart bodies and rights/industry databases when the claim requires them.

## Source-use scores
For each candidate story, the production process may record five separate 0–3 scores rather than one vague 'reliability' number:
- **R = Relevance** to Ben/Music DNA;
- **P = Proximity** to artist/scene (local specialist advantage);
- **A = Authority** for this specific claim;
- **F = Freshness** for time-sensitive claims;
- **D = Depth/originality** (original interview/reporting vs rewrite).

Do not simply sum these into 'truth'. A low-authority but high-proximity fan/archive source can be a superb lead and still require corroboration.

## Weekly search behavior
1. Start from the week's Music-DNA entity/genre map, not from publication homepages alone.
2. Search Tier A by relevant geography/genre.
3. For Scandinavian artists, explicitly search the artist's home-language/local specialist cluster.
4. Add Tier B when the candidate pool or artist origin warrants it.
5. Add Tier C for the strongest stories and historical/visual research.
6. Deduplicate syndicated press-release rewrites; one press release repeated by 15 sites is one information event, not 15-source confirmation.
7. Prefer original interview/review/reporting when several sites repeat the same item.
8. Lock hard facts to primary or sufficiently authoritative evidence before publication.
9. Preserve review disagreement; do not average opinions into fake consensus.
10. Translate/summarize foreign-language reporting into compact original Dutch editorial prose.

## Discovery safeguard
The network must not become a mirror of known favorites. Every week may reserve editorial attention for:
- one local-scene source outside the obvious Anglo/Dutch circuit;
- one specialist source outside the headliner's genre centre;
- one credible discovery that initially sits just outside Ben's established taste boundary.

This is editorial exploration, not a requirement to put the result in the magazine.
