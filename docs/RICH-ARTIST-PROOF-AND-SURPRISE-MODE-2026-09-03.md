# Rich Artist Proof + Verras me! — 2026-09-03

## Status
VALIDATED PRODUCT DIRECTION

## What the H.E.A.T iPhone proof established
The public H.E.A.T rich-artist prototype was tested successfully on iPhone and felt logical, clickable and inviting.

The three-depth grammar works in practice:
- **Kijken** — identify the artist, hear a key track, understand why the artist is in Ben's Music DNA.
- **Bladeren** — story, albums, people, current information.
- **Verdwalen** — follow meaningful connections via people, moods, themes, related artists and other graph routes.

The bottom semantic return path works and must remain guaranteed at every depth.

The H.E.A.T navigation grammar is therefore considered validated enough that it should not be fundamentally redesigned before broader rollout.

## Product principle confirmed by the proof
> **Music DNA hoeft niet alles tegelijk te laten zien om alles bereikbaar te maken.**

Related guardrails:
- richness must never be experienced as complexity;
- every click must answer something or reward curiosity, never merely open another menu;
- music remains the main road, information is a rewarding side path;
- the user must always know how to return.

## Musical resting place
The rich artist world is best understood as a **muzikale pleisterplaats** rather than an information page.

A user may arrive because of one Music DNA relation, stay briefly, listen, read, follow one unexpected connection, and leave again. Nothing needs to be completed.

This creates a core experience loop:

**music -> curiosity -> listening -> connection -> surprise -> more music -> return**

## Spotify as a contextual listening layer
Spotify links are a structural part of the rich-world grammar, not decoration.

Rule:
> **Waar Music DNA een muzikale bewering doet, moet je die waar mogelijk met één tik kunnen beluisteren.**

Examples:
- artist -> listen to artist;
- album -> open album;
- essential track -> open track;
- person connection -> hear a representative track of the connected band;
- Mood/Theme relation -> hear examples from Ben's own music world;
- comparison -> allow Ben to hear both sides of the relation.

Example route:
H.E.A.T -> Erik Grönwall -> Skid Row -> `18 and Life` in Spotify.

Spotify links must be contextual and selective: enough to keep the journey musical, not so many that the page becomes noisy.

## Surprise must be grounded, not random noise
Unexpected links are desirable and can become a characteristic Music DNA experience.

A surprise may be based on:
- Master relation;
- Band/Genre DNA;
- Mood or Theme;
- person/member relation;
- producer/credit relation;
- country;
- period/year;
- style;
- personal rating/learning.

The system may withhold the explanation initially, but a **Waarom krijg ik dit?** / **Waarom deze verbinding?** route must always be available.

## Verras me! — accepted homepage feature
`Verras me!` is accepted as a prominent Music DNA home function.

Purpose:
> Let Music DNA rediscover Ben's own music world when he does not want to decide in advance what to hear.

Default behavior:
- one tap;
- no setup required;
- system selects an artist from the existing Master music world;
- opens that artist's rich musical resting place;
- recent repeats should be reduced so the function stays fresh.

The default selection is **Alles / hoofdindeling** and works immediately.

### Optional direction after the first tap
The user may choose to steer the next surprise by one or more available dimensions:
- hoofdindeling / alles;
- Genre;
- Mood;
- Theme;
- year / period;
- personal taste rating;
- later: country, style, DNA relationship, people, producer/credit and other reliable graph fields.

Combinations are explicitly desirable, e.g.:
- 1970-1979 + Prog Rock;
- Sweden + Hard Rock + 2000+;
- Theatrical + RAAK/GOED;
- Late Night + 1980-1999.

The interface must preserve progressive disclosure: many choices are available, but the user is never required to make them.

## Choice principle
> **Music DNA moet veel keuze bieden, maar nooit voelen alsof je veel móét kiezen.**

And:
> **Music DNA gebruikt de database niet om Ben's keuze te beperken, maar om hem meer manieren te geven om te kiezen.**

The experience therefore supports three levels:
1. choose nothing — `Verras me!`;
2. give a little direction — one filter;
3. play with the database — combine dimensions.

## Important distinction
**Ontdek DNA** finds music that may be new to Ben.

**Verras me!** rediscovers music that is already part of Ben's own music world.

Both may use the same enriched data substrate, but their product purpose is different.

## Database consequence
Every reliable enrichment field should be evaluated against the question:

> **Kan Ben hiermee beter zoeken, kiezen, begrijpen, luisteren of verrast worden?**

If yes, the field creates product value. If not, it is likely ballast.

The database should therefore be as rich as useful, while the interface remains calm.
