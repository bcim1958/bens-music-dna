## 1. New Music DNA learning signal: playlist fit

Ben spontaneously observed that the completed Ontdek DNA playlist for W36 is clearly more enjoyable to him as a whole than W37.

This is not to be treated as a reason to reopen or repair either closed week. It is a new longitudinal learning signal.

### Relevant production difference
W36's delivered gift was created after the recovery flow from 10 positive W36 tracks plus 11 previously verified positive W35 reserve tracks. It therefore passed an additional positive-selection layer before becoming the 21-track playlist.

W37 was built as a fresh 21-track candidate pool combining anchors and controlled adventure routes.

### New distinction
Music DNA must distinguish:

- **track fit** — how Ben values an individual track in the daily listening context;
- **playlist fit** — how strongly the completed 21-track week works as a whole and invites replay.

Playlist fit may depend on properties that are not reducible to the average track rating, including:
- stylistic variety versus clustering;
- energy contour;
- contrast and pacing;
- melodic density;
- ratio of familiar anchors to adventure;
- sequencing;
- avoidance of long runs in a merely 'solid' musical lane;
- the presence of unexpected high-value discoveries.

The W36 > W37 observation is the first explicit comparative playlist-fit signal. It is evidence to learn from, not yet a fixed rule.

### Guardrail
Do not optimize toward 21 guaranteed RAAK tracks. Music DNA must retain controlled uncertainty and discovery. NEE, NOG EENS and unexpected RAAK outcomes remain informative. The aim is not a closed taste bubble, but increasingly intelligent exploration around and beyond Ben's known taste.

### Next analytical use
Future weekly evaluation should preserve both layers separately. W38 and later selection may use the W36/W37 comparison as evidence, but no single two-week comparison may become a hard selection rule without further longitudinal support.

## 2. DNA EXPRESS — editorial autonomy before publication

Normal weekly DNA EXPRESS production is to operate as an autonomous personal editorial desk.

Ben's preferred role is **subscriber/reader**, not pre-publication editor-in-chief.

### Operating rule
Before publication, Music DNA / the DNA EXPRESS editorial process independently decides, among other things:
- which weekly developments deserve attention;
- the cover/headliner;
- whether there is a Dieptebom;
- which Band DNA / Genre DNA story is editorially strongest;
- LIVE! and Flashback emphasis;
- visual worlds and image-led composition;
- issue sequencing and dramaturgy.

Ben should not routinely be asked to approve choices such as the cover artist, Dieptebom subject, image choice or article mix before publication.

### Feedback loop
The intended cadence is:

1. editorial desk prepares and decides independently;
2. Saturday: a completed DNA EXPRESS issue is delivered;
3. Ben reads, discovers and reacts;
4. post-publication feedback informs future issues.

Target notification:

> **Ben, DNA EXPRESS No.xxx ligt voor je klaar.**

No preceding "Akkoord?" is part of the normal production model.

### Editie NUL exception
During Editie NUL, Ben may still be involved where necessary because the editorial desk, visual language, production pipeline and publication machinery themselves are being designed and validated. This temporary build-stage involvement must not become the permanent weekly operating model.

## 3. Repeatability principle

The weekly **process** must become repeatable; the creative **output** must not become templated.

A stable production chain should support:

weekly closed Music DNA snapshot → current/source research → editorial candidate pool → independent story/headliner selection → source verification → visual-world research → issue dramaturgy → copy/assets/build → device QA → publication → post-publication learning → archive.

Repeatability therefore means reliable editorial production, not identical page formulas.
