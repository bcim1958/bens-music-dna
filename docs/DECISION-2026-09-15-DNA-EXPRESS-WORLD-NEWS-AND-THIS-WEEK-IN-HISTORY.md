# DNA EXPRESS — WORLD NEWS + DEZE WEEK IN DE MUZIEKGESCHIEDENIS

Date: 2026-09-15
Status: ACCEPTED EDITORIAL DIRECTION / ACTIVE FOR WEEKLY PRODUCTION

## Why this exists
The expanded Music DNA information network now makes it possible to add two compact, high-value recurring editorial layers to DNA EXPRESS:

1. current music news from across the world;
2. historical music events that happened in the corresponding calendar week across many different years.

Ben explicitly enjoys both. These are therefore not filler. They are part of the personal editorial product.

## 1. NEWS OF THE WORLD
Working rubric title: **NEWS OF THE WORLD**.

Purpose: a fast, pleasurable world scan: short items Ben is likely to enjoy knowing, including things outside the obvious Anglo-American music-news circuit.

### Editorial shape
- normally about 6–10 items, adjusted to the strength of the week;
- deliberately short: typically headline + 1–3 compact sentences;
- international spread where the actual week supports it;
- no country quotas and no obligation to fill weak regions;
- current week/cutoff is strict;
- original/local reporting preferred when the story lives locally;
- primary artist, label, management, promoter or venue source used for hard announcement facts where available;
- specialist press used for interpretation, discovery and scene context;
- syndicated press-release copies are deduplicated into one information event;
- one genuinely surprising item may outrank a larger generic story if it has higher Music-DNA value.

Possible item classes include: new album/single, band formation/reunion/split, personnel change, studio activity, tour/concert/festival, label signing, management/booking change when musically relevant, award/chart milestone, archival discovery/reissue, unusual collaboration, visual/artwork development, scene development, artist statement/interview revelation, death/tribute where editorially important.

The rubric must feel like a world desk, not a feed.

## 2. DEZE WEEK — DOOR DE JAREN
Working rubric title: **DEZE WEEK — DOOR DE JAREN**.

Purpose: collect interesting events that happened in the same calendar week, irrespective of year. This is broader and lighter than the existing deep FLASHBACK feature.

### Editorial distinction
**FLASHBACK** reconstructs one historical story/week in depth and asks what people knew then.

**DEZE WEEK — DOOR DE JAREN** is a compact historical constellation: several dates, several decades, several kinds of event.

They may coexist in one issue and must not duplicate each other unnecessarily.

### Candidate events
- album/single release;
- first/last/important concert;
- band formation, first rehearsal, name change or breakup;
- birth/death when editorially meaningful;
- chart event;
- recording session;
- signing or label event;
- famous/forgotten interview or magazine appearance;
- artwork/photography/stage milestone;
- historic festival/venue event;
- personnel change;
- incident, censorship, controversy or technical breakthrough;
- first encounter/collaboration between later-important musicians;
- an apparently small event whose later significance makes it interesting.

### Shape
Normally 6–10 very short items. Prefer a spread across decades rather than ten facts from one era, but Music-DNA relevance and story quality outrank mathematical balance.

Each item should answer: **what happened, on which date/year, and why is it worth Ben knowing now?**

## Research routing
Both rubrics use the full Artist Information Network and International Source Network.

Current news routing:
artist/official -> label -> management/booking -> venue/promoter/festival -> local specialist press -> international specialist press -> industry press -> radio/podcast/video/original interview.

Historical routing:
contemporaneous press -> venue/festival/archive -> label/artist archive -> radio/audio interview -> historical magazine/fanzine archive -> library/museum/archive -> later retrospective only as supporting/lead material.

Wikipedia/list pages can be discovery indexes, never the sole authority for consequential claims.

## Personalization
These rubrics are not limited to artists already in Ben's database.

Use three concentric circles:
1. directly in Ben's Music DNA;
2. adjacent to it and plausibly interesting;
3. wild-card world discovery with a defensible editorial reason.

This protects discovery and prevents the magazine from becoming an echo chamber.

## Presentation
These sections should be visually fast and magazine-like, not a grid of website cards.
Possible vocabulary: dateline, country/city stamp, tiny map cue, clipped headline, press-wire rhythm, historical date marks, short pull detail.

No decorative filler and no long source apparatus in the reading layer. Provenance stays available in the evidence layer.

## Weekly production protocol insertion
Add to evidence pack before autonomous editorial meeting:
- worldwide current-news sweep using geographic + medium routing;
- same-calendar-week historical sweep across years;
- shortlist and score by relevance, surprise, source quality, freshness/historical proximity, and editorial variety;
- editor selects only the strongest compact items.

These sweeps are standard weekly production tasks from No.001 onward.

## Governance
This adds recurring editorial possibilities; it does not require identical page count or fixed placement every week. The weekly process repeats; the creative issue does not.

No pre-publication approval from Ben is normally required. The editorial desk selects and publishes autonomously under the existing DNA EXPRESS editorial-autonomy decision.

## Principle
**The big story gets room. The small story gets one sharp paragraph. The world is larger than the front page.**
