# Music DNA — Source Map for music journalism and current context

Date: 2026-09-12
Status: ACCEPTED SUPPORTING SOURCE MAP

## Purpose
This document records the source families Music DNA may use for the Personal Weekbrief, Artist Worlds, album/concert context, reviews, interviews, live/tour updates and other living/current music information.

It is not a ranking of publications and it does not mean every source is used for every artist or every question. Source choice is routed by topic, genre, artist, time period and information type.

Core rule: **Music DNA asks not only “is this a reputable source?” but also “is this the right source for this specific claim, period and purpose?”**

## Editorial principles
- Relevance beats volume; no generic rock-news firehose.
- Current/state claims require source + date and must be checked for freshness.
- Historical facts can remain valid without being “refreshed”.
- A new article is a new publication, not automatically evidence of a new current state.
- Official sources are important but not automatically current or complete.
- Fact and opinion stay separate.
- Reviews are opinion/context, not objective truth.
- Multiple reviews may be compared where useful; preserve disagreement.
- Foreign journalism may be offered as a concise Dutch Music-DNA summary with publication, author, date and access to the original source.
- Summaries must be original compact editorial summaries, not full translations or reproductions.
- If reliable information is absent or conflicting, say so rather than fill gaps.

## Structural / reference backbone
### AllMusic
Primary use:
- genre/style/taxonomy backbone;
- artist biography and discography context;
- Similar To / Influenced By / Followed By and other musical relationship clues;
- broad career/album context.

Do not use as sole authority for fast-changing current status, current lineup or live/tour information without corroboration where freshness matters.

### Official artist/band/label sources
Primary use:
- releases;
- announcements;
- official current lineup/context;
- tour announcements;
- direct statements/interviews where published officially.

Caution: official pages can contain stale present-tense biographies, old tour pages or unmaintained information. **Official ≠ automatically current.**

### setlist.fm
Primary use:
- live history;
- setlists;
- song-performance frequency/context;
- concert-date cross-checking.

Treat user-contributed data as useful live-history evidence that may require corroboration for high-confidence historical claims.

### Wikipedia
Primary use:
- orientation and cross-checking;
- broad chronology;
- leads to primary/secondary sources.

Prefer stronger original/secondary sources for consequential claims when available.

### Spotify / Spotify artist information
Primary use:
- operational listening identity and playability;
- current platform positioning and visible artist context;
- direct track/album identity checks.

Spotify is not a replacement for AllMusic taxonomy or editorial/historical sourcing. Normal discography visibility alone does not prove availability/unavailability.

## International music journalism
### Louder / Classic Rock
Strong use for:
- classic rock, hard rock, metal and melodic rock;
- album reviews;
- interviews;
- retrospectives;
- artist features and career context.

Particularly relevant to much of Ben's core taste world.

### Loudwire
Strong use for:
- rock/metal news;
- interviews;
- releases;
- touring/current developments;
- accessible artist features.

Use article date and distinguish news reporting from list/feature/opinion formats.

### Kerrang!
Strong use for:
- rock, punk, alternative and metal;
- interviews;
- reviews;
- contemporary scene/culture context.

### Revolver
Strong use for:
- metal/heavy/alternative artists;
- interviews;
- features;
- current scene context.

### Stereogum
Strong use for:
- indie/alternative/new music;
- release/news context;
- critical/editorial perspective beyond the hard-rock centre.

### Pitchfork
Strong use for:
- album reviews;
- retrospective criticism;
- alternative/indie/experimental context;
- broader critical perspective.

Use as one critic/source among others; do not equate its score/opinion with Music-DNA truth.

## Routing by information question
### “Who/what is this artist musically?”
Start with Master + AllMusic. Use specialist journalism for nuance, reviews and artist-specific context.

### “What is happening now?”
Use dated current journalism + official sources, preferably more than one source when status/lineup/activity is consequential.

### “What did reviewers think of this album?”
Use reputable reviews appropriate to artist/genre and period. Preserve each source's opinion. If useful, Music DNA may synthesize **Wat zeggen de recensenten?** without flattening disagreement.

### “What happened live / when did this concert occur?”
Use setlist.fm, official archives, venue archives and reputable photo/news archives as applicable.

### “What should appear in the Personal Weekbrief?”
Search broadly across the source map, then filter aggressively by Ben's Music DNA. The Weekbrief should contain only items with meaningful personal relevance: releases, reviews, interviews, live/tour developments and worthwhile news. A quiet week may remain quiet.

## Personalization rule
The source map helps Music DNA look outward; it does not decide what Ben should care about.

**The world supplies the evidence. Ben's Music DNA decides what becomes relevant.**

## Maintenance
This source map is extensible. New publications may be added when they repeatedly prove relevant and reliable. Adding a source does not make it a default authority for all claims. Removing or materially changing the role of an accepted core source should be recorded deliberately rather than silently.
